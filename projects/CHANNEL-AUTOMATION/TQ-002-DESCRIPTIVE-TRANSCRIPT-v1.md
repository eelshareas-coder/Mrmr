## 0–8s
Noor watches a video with captions. She asks whether captions alone make the video accessible.

## 8–18s
Noor places a large DONE stamp on the video. The scene presents this as a misconception, not as a judgment against captions.

## 18–32s
Inside the video, an important visual action occurs while the audio discusses something unrelated. The captions accurately represent the speech and meaningful sounds, but the visual action is not represented in the captions.

Noor notices that the movement contains information that is not present in the text.

## 32–48s
A three-panel control board appears with three information channels: sound, text, and visual information. The narrator explains that captions convey speech and relevant non-speech audio, while important visual information may require audio description or an appropriate text-based alternative depending on the media.

## 48–68s
Noor replaces the single “captions = done” checkbox with three checks:
1. Are important speech and sounds represented?
2. Is important visual information represented?
3. Is the alternative appropriate for this content?

## 68–82s
The three channels connect into one accessibility checklist. The narrator explains that captions are an important part of video accessibility, while additional methods depend on the content and information that needs to reach the audience.
