## Evidence Card
Captions are an important accessibility layer because they convey speech and relevant non-speech audio information. They do not automatically communicate important visual information that is absent from the audio; depending on the media, audio description or a text-based alternative may be appropriate.

## Primary Sources
- W3C-CAP-001 — Captions/Subtitles
- W3C-DESC-001 — Description of Visual Information
- W3C-TRANS-001 — Transcripts
- W3C-WCAG-1.2.2 — Captions (Prerecorded)
- W3C-WCAG-1.2.3 — Audio Description or Media Alternative (Prerecorded)
- W3C-WCAG-1.2.5 — Audio Description (Prerecorded)

## Key Claims
1. Captions convey speech and relevant non-speech audio information.
2. Captions do not automatically convey important visual information absent from the audio.
3. Depending on the content, audio description or a descriptive/text alternative can convey important visual information.

## Uncertainty & Boundaries
- Do not say captions are useless.
- Do not say captions alone make every video accessible to everyone.
- Do not imply every video needs the same accessibility package.
- Do not turn general accessibility guidance into individualized advice.

## Misconception / Problem
The misconception is that adding captions completes accessibility for every video regardless of what information the video communicates through visuals.

## Selected Story Mode
myth/fact

## Visual Metaphor
Three channels on a control panel:
- Sound
- Text
- Visual information

The episode shows that adding one channel improves access to some information but does not automatically translate information carried only by another channel.

## Protagonist Action
Noor receives a captioned video and initially marks it "accessible." She then notices that a key visual action is not described in the audio or captions. She changes the production checklist from "captions = done" to a media-specific accessibility check.

## Story Beat Sheet
1. Hook: "لو الفيديو عليه ترجمة… خلاص بقى متاح؟"
2. Myth: Noor checks captions and stamps the video "done."
3. Counterexample: a key visual event happens without being communicated through audio.
4. Evidence reveal: captions cover speech and relevant non-speech audio; visual information may require another alternative.
5. Production lesson: choose accessibility layers based on what information the video actually contains.
6. Takeaway: "الترجمة النصية مهمة، لكنها جزء من إتاحة الفيديو، مش كل الإتاحة."

## Educational Takeaway
"الترجمة النصية مهمة، لكنها تعالج جزءًا من إتاحة الفيديو؛ أحيانًا نحتاج أيضًا إلى تمثيل المعلومات البصرية أو توفير بديل مناسب حسب محتوى الفيديو واحتياج الجمهور."

## Accessibility Plan
- Captions required for the episode itself.
- Important visual information should also be conveyed in the main audio where practical.
- Provide a descriptive text/transcript asset for production QA.
- Keep on-screen text readable and not dependent on color alone.
- QA must check that the episode does not teach the audience a stronger claim than the evidence supports.
