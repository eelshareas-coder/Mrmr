# Research & Evidence Center v1

## Mission
Maintain a discoverable, auditable source library across disability, accessibility, inclusive education, communication, assistive technology, and participation. Convert trustworthy material into distinct episode opportunities without treating source registration as scientific approval.

## Coverage taxonomy
- AAC and multimodal communication
- speech, language, interaction, and communication access
- ADHD, attention, executive function, and learning supports
- positive behaviour support and understanding context/function
- adaptive and inclusive teaching; UDL; school participation
- skill development: daily living, social, learning, mobility, and self-advocacy
- intellectual, developmental, sensory, physical, psychosocial, and multiple disabilities
- assistive technology and accessible digital products
- family, transition, employment, community participation, and rights
- accessibility standards, universal design, and inclusive research

## Team roles
Research Lead; Topic Scout; Source Librarian; Evidence Reviewer; domain reviewer; lived-experience reviewer; Editorial Translator. These are workflow roles, not claims that human specialists have been hired. Clinical, diagnostic, or individualized intervention advice requires qualified human review.

## Source classes
A: official standards, legislation, public health agencies, systematic reviews, peer-reviewed research.
B: universities, professional associations, established specialist organizations, academic books.
C: practitioner resources and reputable explanatory sources.
D: personal accounts and media: valuable for lived experience, not sufficient alone for general factual claims.

## Source intake record
Each source record must include: source_id, title, author/organization, edition/date, source_type, domain_tags, population/context, jurisdiction where relevant, stable URL or bibliographic identifier, access/rights note, review status, reviewer, and reviewed_at.

## Evidence workflow
1. Register source and rights/access status.
2. Check identity, edition, date, author, publisher, and stable location.
3. Read the relevant section; record page/section and exact bounded claim in the team's own words.
4. Classify as research finding, guideline, standard, legal rule, lived experience, interpretation, or opinion.
5. Record limitations, population/context, uncertainty, and contradictory evidence.
6. Seek independent corroboration proportionate to claim impact.
7. Require domain and lived-experience review when applicable.
8. Send only reviewed claims to the claim ledger and editorial decision queue.

## Safety and integrity
- No diagnosis or individualized treatment plans from a video.
- Never imply one support works for everyone.
- Respect autonomy, communication preference, dignity, and consent.
- Distinguish support from cure; avoid deficit-only framing.
- Do not extract or reproduce copyrighted book chapters. Use lawful access and brief notes with page references.
- If evidence is weak, conflicting, outdated, or context-limited, label it and hold claims that would mislead.
